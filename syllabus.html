Exam pattern & syllabus

What the Dr. Homi Bhabha Balvaidnyanik Competition actually asks, and how to prepare for it.

How the competition is structured

Conducted since 1981 by the Greater Bombay Science Teachers' Association for students of Standards 6 and 9, across all boards — SSC, CBSE, ICSE and IB.

RoundWhat it isWho goes through
1 — Written 100 multiple-choice questions, 100 marks, 90 minutes, no negative marking. Physics, Chemistry, Biology and general science awareness. Roughly the top 7.5%
2 — Practical Laboratory tasks: measurement, observation, simple experiments, recording and inference. Shortlisted candidates
3 — Project & interview An action-research project presented and defended in a viva. Final medal decision

The one rule that changes how you write the paper: there is no negative marking. Every blank is a wasted mark. If 90 minutes are nearly up, fill in the remaining bubbles rather than leave them empty.

Awards after the final round: Gold, Silver and Bronze medals with certificates and scholarships; candidates above the qualifying mark receive a passing certificate. Exact cut-offs move every year because selection is a percentage of the field, not a fixed score.

Which books to read

  • Maharashtra State Board, Std 9 Science & Technology — the primary source. Most questions map directly onto these chapters.
  • Maharashtra State Board, Std 7 and 8 Science — the paper freely revisits earlier standards.
  • CBSE / NCERT Science, Classes 6 to 10 — for the extra depth and the numerical style.
  • CISCE (ICSE) Science, Classes 8 and 9 — for topics treated more thoroughly there, especially Physics definitions.
  • Everyday science and current science news — a handful of questions each year reward general awareness.

Broad Standard 9 syllabus

Physics

  • Units, measurement and instruments
  • Motion, laws of motion, force and momentum
  • Gravitation, mass and weight
  • Work, energy and power
  • Pressure, thrust, Archimedes' principle, floatation
  • Heat and its effects
  • Light — reflection, refraction, lenses, the eye
  • Sound — the topic of this practice test
  • Electricity, current, circuits and magnetism
  • Astronomy and space science

Chemistry

  • Matter, its states and properties
  • Atoms, molecules, symbols, valency, formulae
  • Structure of the atom
  • Elements, compounds and mixtures; separation
  • Acids, bases, salts and indicators
  • Metals and non-metals; carbon and its compounds
  • Chemical changes and equations

Biology

  • Cell structure and micro-organisms
  • Tissues in plants and animals
  • Classification of plants and animals
  • Life processes — nutrition, respiration, transport, excretion
  • Human body systems and health
  • Heredity and variation
  • Biotechnology and its uses
  • Ecosystems, environment and pollution
  • Natural resources and their management

General science awareness

  • Scientists and their discoveries
  • SI units, instruments and what they measure
  • Indian space and atomic energy programmes
  • Everyday applications of science
  • Recent science in the news

Sound — chapter checklist

Maharashtra Board Std 9, Chapter 12 Study of Sound, cross-read with CBSE Class 9 Chapter 11 Sound. Tick each item once you can explain it without looking.

  • Sound is produced by a vibrating body; sources in instruments
  • Sound needs a material medium — the bell-jar experiment
  • Sound as a longitudinal mechanical wave
  • Compression and rarefaction; density and pressure variation
  • Amplitude, wavelength, frequency, time period
  • The relation v = f × λ and T = 1/f
  • Speed of sound in solids, liquids and gases
  • Effect of temperature, humidity and pressure on speed
  • Pitch and frequency; loudness and amplitude
  • Loudness ∝ amplitude²; intensity vs loudness; the decibel
  • Quality (timbre); noise and musical sound
  • Laws of reflection of sound; why rough walls still reflect it
  • Echo; persistence of hearing 0.1 s; the 17.2 m minimum
  • Echo numericals — distance, time and speed
  • Reverberation, its control, acoustics of halls
  • Multiple reflection — megaphone, stethoscope, soundboard, ear trumpet, whispering gallery
  • Range of hearing: 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz
  • Infrasound and ultrasound; animals that use each
  • Uses of ultrasound — cleaning, flaw detection, sonography, echocardiography, breaking kidney stones
  • SONAR and echo-ranging numericals
  • Structure and working of the human ear
  • Noise pollution and its control

Echo and SONAR both use the same idea: the sound goes out and comes back, so it covers twice the distance. Distance = (speed × time) ÷ 2. Forgetting the 2 is the commonest mistake in the whole chapter.

How the questions are usually framed

  • Straight recall — a definition, a unit, a value. About a third of the paper.
  • One-step numericals — v = fλ, T = 1/f, echo distance, SONAR depth. Kept simple enough for mental arithmetic.
  • Reason-and-application — "why does X happen?" Bell jar, astronauts on the Moon, why a full hall echoes less.
  • Odd-one-out and NOT questions — read these twice; the word except or not is easy to miss under time pressure.
  • Everyday science — bats, sonography, whispering galleries, noise pollution.

Pace yourself. 100 questions in 90 minutes is 54 seconds each. Answer the recall questions in ten seconds, bank the time, and spend it on the numericals. Never leave a question blank.
